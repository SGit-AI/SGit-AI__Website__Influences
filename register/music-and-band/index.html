Music, and playing in a band

stated practice + era stub

The most personal entry on the site, entirely awaiting the founder's account — and carrying the hypothesis that, if it holds, explains one of the most-traced influences in the register.

Block 1 The anchor

Playing in a band — a personal era

No canonical URL. Not every anchor has one address — a book, a practice or a personal era does not — and this site links what is linkable rather than inventing a canonical home for something that has none. The wider library below carries what can be linked.

Not a work, and not a public event. There is nothing to link, which makes this the only entry on the site with no anchor URL at all.

Block 2 The founder's words

No first-person statement about this influence exists in the corpus yet. What follows is the site's reading of the evidence, not the founder's words — and it stays labelled as such until his briefing document arrives.

The hypothesis is worth stating precisely because of what depends on it. The flow entry is one of the best-traced things in this register: Csikszentmihalyi's state, Victor's mechanism, and a development methodology whose stated core principle is preserving flow. If band-playing is where the founder first experienced that state, then the most-instrumented influence on this site has an origin that is not written down anywhere.

It also has a specific property none of the software influences do: a band is collaborative flow. The methodology tries to recreate a state between a person and a machine. The original may have been a state between people — and that difference, if real, is a design constraint nobody has written down.

Block 3 The principle

(hypothesis) Playing together in real time is the original experience of collaborative flow — several people, immediate feedback, no possibility of stopping to plan.

This is the site's most consequential unverified claim and it is flagged as such everywhere it appears. If the founder confirms it, it becomes the root of a lineage. If he does not, it is a neat story invented by a machine, and the register will say so.

Awaiting the briefing document

This entry is a stub, and says so. Entirely unresearchable by anyone except the founder. There is no public record, no publication to mine and no third party to ask. This entry is a placeholder for something only he can write, and it is published so that its absence is visible rather than tidy.

The research plan

There is no research plan, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest. The entry needs N6 and nothing else. The specific questions worth answering: what the band was and when; whether the flow hypothesis above is right or a machine's tidy story; and whether the collaborative aspect is the part that carried over.

Tracked as N6 in the briefing queue.

The corpus evidence

None. This influence is on the founder's own list and the corpus scan found nothing to attach to it. That is the definition of the STATED tier, and it is published rather than hidden: an empty evidence block is the most honest thing this page can show.

Where this sits

No nesting and no sibling site: this entry stands on its own. The influence map draws every relation the register records.
