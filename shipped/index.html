Tier movement — the changelog

This site has two changelogs. One is the release history, which says what shipped. This one is the interesting one: what changed about the claims — an entry moving tier because a briefing document arrived or the founder confirmed it, a discovered entry struck out because it turned out to be a citation, or a trace row flipping absent to implemented because the estate built one of the gaps an entry specified.

Why this page exists at all. A provenance site whose provenance changed silently would be self-refuting. If an influence quietly moves from the founder said so to and here is the evidence, that is the single most informative event this site can record, and burying it in a diff would waste it.

Gaps the estate could close

The other kind of movement. Every full entry publishes its gaps as build specs, and a gap that gets built flips a trace row from absent to implemented. That turns an influence page into something unusual: a backlog that was written by reading a talk.

None have been closed yet — this is the first release with a register. The ones most worth doing first, on the site's own reading:

  • The methodology does not measure its own central claim. The estate's development methodology says its purpose is preserving flow state, and nothing records whether it does. Interruption count and time-to-first-feedback both fall out of tooling that already exists.
  • The Ive test is scoped to UI, and this estate is mostly not UI. Is it simpler? Would reverting feel worse? applies word for word to an API, a CLI or a file format, and is not asked of any of them.
  • Nothing on this network has ever been publicly retracted. Publishing gaps is the cheap half of falsifiability. Withdrawing a claim in public, with the date, is the expensive half, and no site here has a place to do it.

What would be recorded here if this site were wrong

The transitions above are all additive — entries getting stronger. That is what you would expect from a register whose first release is also its first content release, and it is also exactly what you would expect from a site that only ever confirms itself.

So, precisely: a DISCOVERED → removed row would appear here if the founder said an entry was a citation rather than an influence, naming what was struck and why. A retraction row would appear if a trace row turned out to be wrong. A counter-influence entry would appear in the register if there is something everyone assumes shaped the work and did not. None of those has happened, and until one does, this page is evidence of ambition rather than of practice. The disclosure page says the same thing at more length.

influences.sgit.ai

A site in the sgit.ai network: the people, works, topics and things that shaped the founder's thinking, and the places in the estate where that shaping can be checked. An influence entry here is a falsifiable claim about the codebase, not a reading list. The site's own words are CC BY 4.0; the anchor works belong to their authors and are linked, never rehosted.
